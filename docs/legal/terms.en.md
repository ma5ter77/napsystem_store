Check compliance with total-price disclosure rules (EU: Directives 98/6/EC and 2011/83/EC require consumer prices inclusive of taxes; Russia: Article 10 of the Consumer Protection Law, price in roubles inclusive of taxes; Serbia — similar rules), EU OSS rules for electronic services, Russian VAT on e-services by foreign suppliers (Article 174.2 of the Tax Code), and Serbian PDV. Displaying VAT-inclusive final prices will likely be required.
6. **Consumer withdrawal for subscriptions.** For the EU/Serbia, the right is lost with express consent and acknowledgment — check whether Article 16(m) covers a subscription treated as a "digital service" (Directive 2019/770 distinguishes digital content from digital services; for services Article 16(a) and pro rata payment apply). For Russia — application of Article 32 of the Consumer Protection Law and refunds for unused periods, reconciled with the business rule "cancel — access until end of period". Check implementation of the withdrawal button (currently withdrawal is made through the refund request with the option "Withdrawal from the contract (no reason needed)") (Article 11a of Directive 2011/83/EU as amended by Directive 2023/2673) and its transposition in Serbia.
7. **Age.** The simplified rule "18+; younger only via a parent's account" is used. Check: Russia — partial capacity of minors aged 14–18 (Article 26 of the Civil Code); EU/Serbia — age of digital consent (Article 8 GDPR, 16; Serbian ZZPL — 15). Consider whether age verification is required.
8. **Governing law clause enforceability.** The choice of Serbian law for EU and Russian consumers is limited by Article 6 of Rome I and Article 1212 of the Russian Civil Code / Article 16 of the Consumer Protection Law (unfair terms). Check enforceability of the jurisdiction clause and the complaint procedure (a mandatory pre-trial procedure for Russian consumers may be held to infringe their rights).
9. **Limitation of liability and "as is".** Check clauses 13.2–13.4 against Article 16 of the Russian Consumer Protection Law and Directive 93/13/EEC on unfair terms.
10. **Unilateral amendment.** For Russian consumers, a unilateral change of terms may be an unfair term (Article 16 of the Consumer Protection Law, Article 310 of the Civil Code). Check the 14-day notice mechanism and how consent is obtained.
11. **Hardware returns in Russia.** Deduction of return shipping costs; applicability of the list of non-returnable goods (headphones — hygiene?); whether any items are technically complex goods.
12. **Developer and Supplier agreements.** The text assumes they exist (clauses 1.5, 4.1, 4.7); separate documents are needed, including warranties of content rights and a ban on medical claims.
13. **Account deletion and access to purchases.** Clause 3.7 ends access to purchased content on deletion — check whether export / additional warning is needed and whether this is an unfair term.
14. **Sanctions and cross-border settlements** between Serbia, the EU and Russia (Stripe and ЮKassa) — check the effect on enforceability and the wording of clause 13.6.
15. **Reviews.** Check Directive (EU) 2019/2161 requirements to disclose how review authenticity is verified, and Digital Services Act obligations for marketplaces (notice-and-action, statements of reasons for moderation, trader traceability under Article 30 DSA).
16. **Serbian version.** Check whether a Serbian-language version is required for consumers in Serbia.
17. **Digital order confirmation.** Implemented: the payment e-mail for a digital purchase repeats the consent to immediate access and the acknowledgment of loss of the right of withdrawal and states the Terms version (Article 8(7) of Directive 2011/83/EU). Check that the wording is sufficient.
18. **VAT-inclusive prices.** Move to VAT-inclusive prices for consumers (Directives 98/6/EC, 2011/83/EU Article 6(1)(e), Article 10 of the Russian Consumer Protection Law).
